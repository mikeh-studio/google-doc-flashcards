# Habit Systems for Focused Study

> [!summary]
> A neutral sample deck about study routines, spaced review, and focused flashcard design.

- Source: examples/sample-source.md
- Source type: file
- Created: 2026-06-02T00:00:00+00:00
- Cards: 10
- Generator: sample

## Cards

### Card 1

> [!question]
> Why should a study system make the next action obvious before the session begins?

> [!success] Answer
> It prevents the learner from spending attention on setup decisions instead of studying.

> [!note] Explanation
> Reducing setup friction makes the study routine easier to repeat.

**Source cue:** A better system makes the next action obvious before the session begins.

**Tags:** study_systems, focus

### Card 2

> [!question]
> What are three parts of a repeatable focused study system?

> [!success] Answer
> The environment, the plan, and the feedback loop.

> [!note] Explanation
> These parts shape whether the learner can start, continue, and improve future sessions.

**Source cue:** Focused study works best when the environment, the plan, and the feedback loop are simple enough to repeat.

**Tags:** study_systems, recall

### Card 3

> [!question]
> What should a learner choose at the start of a focused review session?

> [!success] Answer
> A single review target, a short timer, and a concrete expected outcome.

> [!note] Explanation
> A narrow target and clear outcome make the session easier to complete and evaluate.

**Source cue:** choose a single review target, set a short timer, and write down the expected outcome

**Tags:** planning, focus

### Card 4

> [!question]
> Give an example of a concrete expected outcome for a study session.

> [!success] Answer
> Explain three causes of an event or solve five problems without notes.

> [!note] Explanation
> Concrete outcomes are easier to check than vague goals like studying more.

**Source cue:** explain the three causes of the event or solve five problems without notes

**Tags:** planning, examples

### Card 5

> [!question]
> Why does spaced review improve long-term memory?

> [!success] Answer
> It repeats recall after some forgetting has happened, forcing reconstruction of the answer.

> [!note] Explanation
> Reconstructing an answer is stronger practice than simply recognizing it immediately.

**Source cue:** memory improves when recall is repeated after some forgetting has happened

**Tags:** memory, spacing

### Card 6

> [!question]
> Why can reviewing one card ten times in one sitting be misleading?

> [!success] Answer
> It can feel productive while producing weaker long-term retention than spaced review.

> [!note] Explanation
> Immediate repetition may rely on recognition instead of durable recall.

**Source cue:** Reviewing the same card ten times in one sitting can feel productive

**Tags:** memory, spacing

### Card 7

> [!question]
> What makes a flashcard easier to grade?

> [!success] Answer
> It asks for one clear idea at a time.

> [!note] Explanation
> Single-idea cards reduce ambiguity and make correct/again decisions more reliable.

**Source cue:** Good flashcards ask for one idea at a time.

**Tags:** flashcards, design

### Card 8

> [!question]
> What is wrong with a flashcard that asks for five facts at once?

> [!success] Answer
> It is hard to grade and easy to avoid.

> [!note] Explanation
> Multi-part cards blur whether the learner actually knows the material.

**Source cue:** A card that asks for five facts at once is hard to grade and easy to avoid.

**Tags:** flashcards, design

### Card 9

> [!question]
> What should happen to cards marked again?

> [!success] Answer
> They should return sooner in the review schedule.

> [!note] Explanation
> An again mark is feedback that the next repetition should happen earlier.

**Source cue:** Cards marked "again" should return sooner

**Tags:** feedback, review

### Card 10

> [!question]
> What is the purpose of a review session?

> [!success] Answer
> To find the edge of forgetting and make the next repetition more useful.

> [!note] Explanation
> Review is most valuable when it reveals what needs attention next.

**Source cue:** find the edge of forgetting and make the next repetition more useful

**Tags:** feedback, review
